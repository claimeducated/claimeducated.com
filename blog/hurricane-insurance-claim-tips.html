Hurricane Season: How to File a Property Claim After a Storm

Storm damage claims are complex and time-sensitive. Here is what to do, in order, after a hurricane or major wind event damages your home.

8 min read  ·  March 1, 2025

Hurricanes and major windstorms create a different kind of insurance claim. Unlike isolated losses, storm events often involve widespread regional damage, contractor shortages, delayed inspections, emergency mitigation, power outages, roof exposure, flooding concerns, and thousands of simultaneous claims. That combination creates pressure, confusion, and urgency.

And unfortunately, many homeowners accidentally damage their own claim in the chaotic days immediately after a storm. Here is how to approach a hurricane or major storm damage claim strategically, calmly, and in the right order.

1. Prioritize Safety First

Before thinking about insurance, make sure everyone is safe. Avoid downed power lines, watch for structural instability, and avoid entering severely damaged areas until conditions are clear. After hurricanes especially, additional hazards may include gas leaks, contaminated floodwater, unstable trees, exposed wiring, and compromised roofing. If authorities advise evacuation or restricted access, follow those instructions.

Property can be repaired. Safety comes first.

2. Prevent Additional Damage Immediately

Most insurance policies require homeowners to take reasonable steps to prevent further damage after a loss. This is called mitigation. Examples include tarping roof openings, boarding broken windows, removing standing water, drying wet materials, or shutting off utilities if necessary.

Do not wait days for the insurance company before protecting exposed areas if damage is worsening. Storm claims move quickly, and additional preventable damage can complicate coverage discussions later.

3. Document Everything Before Cleanup

This step is critical. Before removing debris or beginning major cleanup, photograph the entire property, document all affected areas, and preserve evidence thoroughly. Take wide-angle photos, close-up damage photos, and video walkthroughs. Document roofing damage, siding, windows, ceilings, water intrusion, fallen trees, fences, detached structures, and damaged belongings.

Storm conditions change quickly after cleanup begins. Your original documentation may become extremely important later.

4. Separate Wind Damage From Flood Damage

This becomes especially important after hurricanes. Many homeowners policies typically cover wind-driven damage, rain entering through storm-created openings, and certain storm-related losses. But flood damage may require separate flood insurance coverage.

Because multiple causes of loss can occur simultaneously during hurricanes, documentation matters enormously. Photograph water lines, storm openings, roof damage, broken windows, and points of water entry carefully. Cause-of-loss distinctions can significantly affect claim handling.

5. Contact Your Insurance Company Promptly

Report the claim as soon as reasonably possible. Provide your policy number, date of loss, basic description of damage, and emergency mitigation steps already taken. Keep communication factual and concise. Avoid guessing about full repair costs, speculating about causes, or minimizing damage before inspections occur. If you do not yet know the full extent of damage, say so. That is normal after storms.

6. Start a Claim Documentation System Immediately

Storm claims generate a large amount of paperwork quickly. Create folders for photos, videos, receipts, contractor estimates, mitigation invoices, hotel costs, adjuster communications, and claim correspondence. Track dates, phone calls, names, and summaries of conversations. Organization becomes extremely valuable during catastrophe claims.

7. Understand That Initial Inspections May Be Fast

After major hurricanes, adjusters often handle extremely high claim volumes across large geographic areas and heavily damaged neighborhoods. That means inspections may initially be shorter than homeowners expect. Do not assume every issue will automatically be identified.

Walk through damage carefully if possible and point out interior water intrusion, hidden moisture concerns, detached materials, attic staining, and damaged personal property. Your documentation supports the process.

8. Be Careful With Emergency Contractors

After storms, neighborhoods are often flooded with roofers, mitigation companies, public adjusters, and traveling contractors. Some are excellent. Some are not. Do not rush into large deposits, vague contracts, assignment agreements, or pressure-based decisions. Read documents carefully and research licensing and reputation when possible. Storm stress makes homeowners vulnerable to rushed decisions.

9. Save Every Receipt

Keep receipts for tarps, emergency repairs, generators, fuel, hotel stays, meals if covered, temporary housing, storage, and mitigation equipment. Additional living expenses may be partially reimbursable depending on policy terms. Without documentation, reimbursement becomes harder.

10. Expect Hidden Damage Possibilities

Storm damage is not always fully visible immediately. Especially after hurricanes, hidden issues may include moisture behind walls, roof deck damage, insulation saturation, HVAC damage, mold development, or structural shifting. This is why supplements sometimes become necessary later in the claim process. Continue documenting as repairs progress.

11. Understand Your Deductible Before Assuming Payment Amounts

Hurricane and wind deductibles are often different from standard AOP deductibles. Many storm deductibles are percentage-based rather than flat dollar amounts. For example, a 2% hurricane deductible on a $500,000 dwelling limit equals $10,000 out of pocket. This surprises many homeowners after major storms. Review your declarations page carefully before making financial assumptions.

12. Do Not Throw Away Damaged Items Too Quickly

Preserve damaged roofing materials, flooring, appliances, furniture, electronics, and storm-damaged belongings whenever practical. If disposal becomes necessary, photograph thoroughly first, document why disposal occurred, and preserve representative samples if possible. Evidence matters.

13. Be Patient but Stay Organized

Major catastrophe claims often move slower than standard claims because claim volume is massive, material shortages occur, inspections take time, and contractor availability becomes limited. Staying organized helps reduce stress while the process unfolds. Continue documenting, following up professionally, and maintaining written records.

14. Watch for Mold and Secondary Damage

Warm, humid hurricane environments create high mold risk. Dry affected areas quickly whenever possible. Monitor odors, discoloration, humidity, and worsening moisture conditions. Document changes immediately. Secondary damage can escalate rapidly after storms.

15. Read Your Policy, Especially the Storm-Related Sections

Most homeowners never read their hurricane deductibles, wind exclusions, water exclusions, ordinance and law provisions, or loss settlement language until after damage happens. Understanding what is covered, what may be excluded, and what obligations exist after a loss helps reduce confusion later.

Final Thought

Hurricane claims are emotionally exhausting. There is often displacement, uncertainty, contractor pressure, financial stress, and physical damage happening all at once. That is why organization matters so much.

The homeowners who generally navigate storm claims most effectively are not necessarily the loudest or most aggressive. They are usually the ones who are calm, documented, organized, and informed. Because after a major storm:

clarity becomes one of the most valuable tools you have.
